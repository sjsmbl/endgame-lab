Endgame Academy

A guided curriculum from basic checkmates to difficult theoretical endings. Mate Trainer currently cycles through its built-in puzzle list; it does not unlock new puzzles as you solve them. Endgame Puzzles uses the JSON datasets in your puzzles folder, while these lessons teach the core ideas in plain language.

1. King + queen vs king

Beginner В· Basic checkmate

Use your queen to shrink the enemy king's box, then bring your own king closer. Avoid stalemate: if the enemy king has no legal move but is not in check, the game is a draw.

2. King + rook vs king

Beginner В· Basic checkmate

Cut the king off with your rook, use your king to take away escape squares, and push the enemy king to an edge. Keep the rook protected and avoid allowing a stalemate.

3. Two bishops vs king

Intermediate В· The bishop pair

The bishops control diagonals of both colours. Work with your king to drive the enemy king toward a corner, then use the bishops to cover the escape squares while delivering mate.

4. Bishop + knight vs king

Advanced В· The difficult basic mate

This is a forced checkmate but requires precision. The bishop controls one colour complex; the knight helps cover the other colour's escape squares. Drive the king to a corner that matches your bishop's colour, using a repeatable W-shaped knight manoeuvre.

5. King + pawn vs king: opposition

Beginner В· Pawn endings

Opposition means the kings face each other with one square between them. The side not to move often has the opposition. Use it to escort your pawn forward, but check whether the king can reach the promotion square in time.

6. The rule of the square

Beginner В· Pawn race

Imagine a square from the pawn to its promotion square. If the defending king can enter that square when it is the king's turn to move, it can usually catch the pawn; otherwise the pawn promotes. Account for the pawn's two-square first move.

7. Rook endgames: activate the rook

Intermediate В· Rook endings

Active rooks usually matter more than passive pawn-grabbing. Put the rook behind passed pawns, cut off the enemy king when possible, and activate your king. Rook endgames often require checking from behind rather than blocking the pawn directly.

8. Philidor defence

Advanced В· Rook + pawn vs rook

In the classic rook-and-pawn-versus-rook setup, the defender can often draw by keeping the king in front of the pawn and the rook on the third rank until the pawn advances, then checking from behind or the side. Exact positions matter.

9. Lucena position

Advanced В· Rook + pawn vs rook

A classic winning setup for the stronger side with a rook pawn or central pawn on the seventh rank and the king supporting it. The usual idea is to build a bridge with the rook to shield the king from checks while the pawn promotes.

10. Rook vs rook: draw-saving technique

Advanced В· Defensive technique

Many rook endings are drawable with accurate defence. Look for checking distance, side checks, a king in front of the pawn, and whether the stronger side can escape perpetual checks. This is not one universal position: the pawn's file and rank change the result.

11. Bishop vs knight

Intermediate В· Minor-piece endings

Bishops are strongest when play exists on both wings and pawns are on both colours. Knights can be strong with blocked pawns and fixed targets. Evaluate pawn structure, king activity, and whether the bishop is trapped behind its own pawns.

12. Queen vs king: avoid stalemate

Beginner В· Conversion technique

Keep the opposing king boxed in without taking away every move unless the king is in check. Bring your king close before the final net, and always verify the position is checkmate rather than stalemate.
